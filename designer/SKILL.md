# Visual Design Principles (Storytelling with Data)

*Adapted from Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic's "10 Principles for Better Visual Design"*

Design is a cultivated skill, not an innate talent. Effective visual communication is a function of attention, deliberate constraints, visual hierarchy, and knowing what to look for.

---

## The 10 Core Principles

### 1. Start with your audience, not your content
- **Rule**: Before placing a single node, box, or pixel, answer: *Who will see this, and what single takeaway or decision do they need from it?*
- Avoid starting from creator-centric convenience (dumping all internal state/code); design strictly for viewer comprehension.

### 2. Don't let the default be your design
- **Rule**: Software defaults (colors, fonts, line weights, chart types, auto-layouts) are unconsidered generic decisions.
- Explicitly choose typography, palettes, margins, and node boundaries tailored to the specific problem.

### 3. Constrain your choices (Less is More)
- **Rule**: Restrict variables to eliminate visual cacophony.
  - Palette: Start with 1–2 intentional accent colors on a neutral background;
    add more only when data categories, accessibility, brand, or meaning require it.
  - Typography: Prefer one family with consistent weights; use another when
    language support, readability, or an established design system requires it.
  - Shapes: Keep equivalent containers consistent; vary shapes only to encode a
    real distinction.

### 4. Create clear visual hierarchy
- **Rule**: Direct the viewer's eye deliberately through size, weight, position, and spacing.
  - **Level 1 (Primary)**: What must be noticed first (key bottleneck, primary flow, core result).
  - **Level 2 (Secondary)**: Supporting stages or categories.
  - **Level 3 (Tertiary)**: Details, annotations, and metadata.

### 5. Understand that difference signals meaning
- **Rule**: Every visual difference (color shift, bold text, dashed line, larger box) is interpreted by the viewer as meaningful signal.
  - If elements share the same role and importance, keep their treatment
    consistent unless another encoded distinction requires variation.
  - Eliminate accidental variations in padding, font sizes, or border styles.

### 6. Use color with purpose
- **Rule**: Treat color as a scarce, high-value visual cue.
  - Start with neutral tones for structure and baseline content, then respect an
    established brand or atmosphere when one is part of the brief.
  - Use vibrant color deliberately for attention, data categories, accessibility,
    brand, or mood rather than as arbitrary variation.

### 7. Align everything thoughtfully
- **Rule**: Misalignment creates subtle visual unease and amateurism.
  - Anchor every element to a grid, edge, or shared axis.
  - Align multi-line text to its language's reading direction rather than
    centering it by default; use right alignment for Persian and other RTL text.
  - Keep connector lines clean (straight orthogonal lines or simple uniform arrows).

### 8. Give things room to breathe (Whitespace is Active)
- **Rule**: Empty space is functional. It groups related elements, separates distinct steps, and prevents cognitive overload.
  - Resist the urge to pack every corner.
  - Generous padding and step spacing make complex flows feel calm, clear, and easy to parse.

### 9. Seek inspiration and analyze both good and bad design
- **Rule**: Actively dissect why effective diagrams work (layout, contrast, typography) and identify what fails in cluttered designs.

### 10. Trust your eyes, then train them
- **Rule**: If a diagram feels visually cluttered, awkward, or hard to read at a glance, trust that instinct and refactor it immediately.

## Agent Action Checklist for Visual Artifacts

1. [ ] **Audience Check**: Is the primary takeaway apparent quickly to its intended audience?
2. [ ] **Palette Restraint**: Is every accent color carrying information or meeting an accessibility/brand need?
3. [ ] **Hierarchy & Meaning**: Does every color or size difference convey a meaningful, deliberate distinction?
4. [ ] **Alignment**: Are elements aligned to a consistent grid and is text aligned to its reading direction?
5. [ ] **Whitespace**: Is there sufficient breathing room between steps and containers?
